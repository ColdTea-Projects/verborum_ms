1. THE STATEMENT BEING EXPLAINED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Earlier I wrote:

  "Giving a contributor in Azerbaijan access to real user data is a
   transfer to a third country with no adequacy decision. Keep them on
   seed and test data only, using scripts/dev-seed. Otherwise you need
   written agreements (SCCs plus confidentiality)."

This file explains each part of that statement.


2. SHORT ANSWER
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Verborum has users in the EU. The GDPR applies to their data.
- The GDPR limits the movement of personal data to countries outside
  the EU/EEA (Chapter V, Articles 44 to 49).
- Azerbaijan has no EU adequacy decision [CHECK the current list].
- Remote access from Azerbaijan to a database in the EU is a transfer.
  The data does not need to be copied or downloaded. Viewing it is
  enough.
- The simple, safe solution: your partner works only on fake data.
  Then no personal data goes to Azerbaijan and Chapter V does not apply.
- The other solution: signed agreements and technical limits. This
  costs time and money, and you stay responsible.


3. WHAT IS "PERSONAL DATA" IN VERBORUM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Personal data is any information about an identifiable person
(Article 4(1)). In Verborum this includes:

  Keycloak            e-mail address, name, login history, linked
                      Google or Facebook identity, password hashes
  ms_user             display name, profile, vault, learning statistics,
                      marketplace agreement
  ms_dictionary       dictionaries and words. A user can type anything
                      here, so these texts can hold personal data
  ms_marketplace      listings, ratings, import history, linked to a user
  ms_notification     push device tokens and X-Device-Id values
  RabbitMQ            events with the Keycloak id of the user
  Logs and backups    IP addresses, user ids, error messages with data
  Database dumps      all of the above in one file

Pseudonymous data (a Keycloak id without a name) is still personal data
if you can link it to a person. Removing the name does not remove the
duty.


4. WHAT IS A "TRANSFER TO A THIRD COUNTRY"
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The GDPR has no definition, but the European Data Protection Board
(Guidelines 05/2021) gives three conditions:

  1. A controller or processor (the exporter) is subject to the GDPR
     for the processing.
  2. The exporter discloses or makes the data available to another
     party (the importer).
  3. The importer is in a third country.

"Makes available" includes remote access. These actions are transfers
if the person sits in Azerbaijan:

  - A login to the production database or to Adminer
  - A view of the Keycloak admin console
  - A copy of a database dump or a backup
  - A shared screen with real user rows
  - A log file with user ids or IP addresses sent by e-mail or chat
  - A screenshot of a real account in a bug report

The servers can be in Germany. This does not change the result. The
location of the person who reads the data is what matters.


5. WHY AZERBAIJAN IS A PROBLEM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Article 45: a transfer is free if the European Commission has decided
that the country gives an "adequate" level of protection.

Countries with an adequacy decision (as I know the list; [CHECK]):
Andorra, Argentina, Canada (commercial organizations), Faroe Islands,
Guernsey, Israel, Isle of Man, Japan, Jersey, New Zealand, South Korea,
Switzerland, United Kingdom, Uruguay, and the United States for
companies in the Data Privacy Framework.

Azerbaijan is not on this list.

Azerbaijan has its own personal-data law, and I believe it is a party
to Council of Europe Convention 108 [CHECK]. This is useful context.
It is NOT an adequacy decision. A national law or Convention 108
membership does not replace Article 46 safeguards.


6. WHEN NO ADEQUACY DECISION EXISTS: THE OPTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Article 46 safeguards (the normal route):

  A. Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), Commission Decision
     2021/914. You and your partner sign them.
       - Module 2: controller to processor. Use this if your partner
         works on your instructions. This is the usual case.
       - Module 1: controller to controller. Use this if your partner
         decides on purposes and means too.
  B. A transfer impact assessment (TIA). After the Schrems II ruling
     (CJEU C-311/18) you must check whether the laws of Azerbaijan,
     for example access by public authorities, make the SCCs
     ineffective in practice. You write down the result.
  C. Supplementary measures if the TIA shows a gap, for example
     encryption where only you hold the key, or access to pseudonymized
     data only.

Article 28 (a processor agreement) is ALSO needed if your partner is a
processor. It must say, among other things:

  - Processing only on your documented instructions
  - A duty of confidentiality for the persons who access the data
  - Security measures (Article 32)
  - Help with data-subject rights and breach notices
  - Delete or return the data at the end
  - Rules for sub-processors
  - Audit rights

The SCCs (module 2) already contain most of these terms. Many
controllers still add a short separate agreement for the practical
rules.

Article 49 derogations (consent, contract necessity, and so on) are
exceptions. The EDPB says to use them only for occasional transfers.
A partner with regular access is not occasional. Do not use Article 49
for this case.


7. WHAT IS YOUR PARTNER, LEGALLY?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The answer changes the paperwork. Decide first.

  Employee or contractor who follows your instructions
    -> Processor (Article 28). Use SCCs module 2 plus the processor
       agreement.

  Co-owner who decides with you what the app does with user data
    -> Joint controller (Article 26). You need an arrangement that
       states who does what. The SCCs module 1 may also apply for the
       transfer. Users can ask about the essence of this arrangement.

  A person who helps without any contract
    -> The law does not accept this as a safe position. You stay the
       controller, and you gave the data to someone with no agreement.
       This is the worst case.

The "household exemption" (Article 2(2)(c)) does NOT apply. Verborum is
a public service with users who are not your family or friends.

Important: you are a private person and the controller. You are
personally liable. There is no company that holds the risk.


8. THE RISKS IF YOU DO NOTHING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Fines (Article 83(5)): up to EUR 20 million or 4 percent of
    annual turnover for transfer violations. A small project gets
    smaller fines in practice, but the legal maximum is real.
  - A user can claim damages (Article 82).
  - A complaint to a supervisory authority is free for the user.
  - If the data leaks at your partner's side, you must still notify the
    authority within 72 hours (Article 33), and you must tell the
    users if the risk is high (Article 34).
  - Apple and Google can ask for your privacy practices. A false
    answer in the privacy label is a store-policy problem.
  - You must show that you comply (Article 5(2), accountability). With
    no documents, you cannot.


9. RECOMMENDED SOLUTION: SEED AND TEST DATA ONLY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Your partner never touches production. This avoids the transfer
completely. No SCCs, no TIA.

How to do it in this repository:

  1. Use scripts/dev-seed/seed.py. It creates 30 fake users through the
     real APIs, with dictionaries, Forum membership, imports and
     ratings. reset.py removes them.
  2. The local stack (docker compose) holds only fake data. Your
     partner runs it on their own computer.
  3. Dev accounts testuser and testadmin are fake. They are fine.
  4. Real e-mail addresses are NOT used in seed data. Check that
     seed.py uses invented addresses. [CHECK before you share]
  5. Your partner gets NO access to:
       - the production server, database, Adminer, RabbitMQ UI
       - the production Keycloak admin console
       - backups, dumps, log files, error-tracker data
       - the production .env file and secrets
  6. A bug report from a real user: you remove names, e-mail
     addresses, ids and IP addresses BEFORE you send it. Or you
     reproduce the bug with seed data.
  7. Git: the repository must hold no real data. See section 10.
  8. Access to the code repository itself is not a transfer of user
     data. Secrets in the repository would be a problem. The project
     rule "no hardcoded secrets" already covers this.
  9. A Verborum production fix that needs real data: YOU do it, or you
     ask your partner to write the fix and you run it.

Why this is the best option for a small project:
  - No contract negotiation or legal fees
  - No TIA to maintain
  - Less risk in a breach
  - It is also good engineering practice (least privilege)

11. IF YOUR PARTNER MUST HAVE REAL DATA
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Use this only if a real need exists. Do these steps in this order:

  1. Decide the role (section 7).
  2. Reduce the data. Give only what the task needs (Article 5(1)(c)).
     Use a dump with names, e-mail addresses and free text removed or
     replaced.
  3. Sign the SCCs (module 2 or 1) and the processor agreement
     (Article 28). Include a confidentiality duty in writing.
  4. Write the TIA for Azerbaijan. Keep it with your records.
  5. Set technical limits:
       - A separate read-only database role, no write access
       - Access through a VPN or a bastion host, with logs
       - Individual accounts, no shared password
       - Disk encryption and a screen lock on your partner's computer
       - No local copies of the data. Delete when done.
       - Remove access when the task ends
  6. Add the processor and the third-country transfer to:
       - Your record of processing activities (Article 30)
       - Your privacy policy (Articles 13 and 14 name recipients and
         transfers)
  7. Review the arrangement once a year.

This is a lot of work for one person. This is why section 9 is the
recommendation.


12. OTHER POINTS TO REMEMBER
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Other law: Azerbaijan may have its own rules. Sanctions and export
    rules are not a topic here, but a lawyer can check them in the same
    session. [CHECK]
  - Employment law: if your partner is an employee, German or local
    labor and tax law applies. This is outside the data-protection
    question.
  - Hosting: if the servers are with a provider in the EU, you also need
    an Article 28 agreement with that provider. Most providers offer one
    online.
  - Keycloak: a Keycloak admin account in the hands of your partner is
    "access to real users", even if the partner never exports a list.
  - Developer tools: crash reporting, analytics and log services that you
    add later can send data to other countries. Check each one.
